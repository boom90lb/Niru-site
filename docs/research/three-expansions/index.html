The three expansions of the search

AMLO’s design widens what it optimizes in three stages, from architectures to training programs to the deployed model with its harness.

Most architecture search holds the training recipe fixed and leaves the deployed system out of scope. In AMLO’s design, the architecture is only the first object of the search. The program widens that object in three stages, with the same accounting at each.

Architecture

The first stage is an evolutionary search over architectures. Each candidate is a typed graph of components, and what the graph means is fixed before it runs. Before training, a candidate gets a structural check and an analytic cost estimate. It then trains under a fixed compute budget and advances through the evaluation tiers only when the evidence supports it. This stage is the reference path. Parts of its foundation are built, including the checks before training, the compute planner and the promotion statistics. Candidate training inside the search is the next milestone.

Training programs

The second stage puts the training program into the search, with its data mixtures and their schedules, filters, the order of the data, objectives, optimizers and the phases of training. These choices can move results as much as the architecture does; one study of architecture search found the training protocol mattered more. They are also where the most specialist knowledge sits. A program is a candidate in its own right, with its own identity and record. Once the search covers it under the same budgets and gates, it can aim at an outcome rather than at a network. A training-program study can run at one scale; it needs a test of transfer only when its claim is about another scale.

The deployed model and its harness

The third stage widens the boundary once more, to the system a user meets, which is the model with its tools, memory, retrieval, verifiers and the inference budget it is allowed to spend. At this stage the search optimizes a working system, and it can include the loop that trains that system.

Where the search runs

Distributed training is a separate line of work. It changes where the search runs, not what it optimizes. Placement and sharding become recorded choices, and the identities, budgets and gates stay the same.

Why staged, not joint

The three stages are coupled. An architecture can change which curriculum is best. A curriculum can change what a harness is worth. A deployment context can invalidate a result from an earlier stage. AMLO neither ignores these effects nor searches everything at once. It optimizes in stages, gives each stage a typed contract with the next, and measures the interactions directly. A pipeline is trusted only inside the region where that measurement supports it, and where the interaction is large, the stages are searched together with their own budget. Each stage keeps the full record of the stage before it.

Beyond the three stages

The long-term aim is a system that applies the same procedure to its own method. It proposes a change to how it searches, and keeps the change only when a sealed comparison supports it. This is a research aim, not a stage of the current plan. It needs its own evidence that each change to the method helps, measured against a reference the method cannot alter.

The distinctive bet is in the combination of an editable representation of the whole learning program, compositional meaning and cost, small-scale experiments with tested transfer, and independent confirmation under declared resources. A research system could then learn which experiments are worth running, while its proposed changes to the research method face the same comparison discipline as changes to a model.

The potential advantage is cumulative. An improvement in how research is conducted can help find subsequent improvements in the learner, training program, and working system. That is a reason to pursue this line of attack, not evidence that the loop will be stable or that its benefits will compound without limit. Each of those claims needs its own experiments. Confirmation must remain outside the process being optimized; a method cannot improve its result by rewriting the test that judges it.
